Hackathon project · explained simply

Mohar

A record of who held the exam paper, and when, that nobody can quietly change afterwards — including us.

1The problem

Question papers leak before exams. It happens often enough that most people have stopped being surprised by it.

Here is the thing most people get wrong about why. Papers do not leak because someone cracked a password or broke the encryption. They leak because a person who was allowed to hold the paper gave it to someone else.

In the Hazaribagh case, the school principal was authorised to be in the strong room. There was no lock to break. What was missing was any record of who had the package and when. The register was written up afterwards, by hand, and it said whatever was convenient.

So the real question is

Not "how do we stop a leak" — nothing can promise that. It is: when a leak happens, can we prove who held the paper at every moment? And can that proof survive the people who would most like to change it?

2Our idea, in one line

Every step in the paper's journey is written down as a signed record, in a chain, where nothing can be edited or removed later — not even by us.

Three plain ideas make that work.

Every record is signed

Each device — a phone, a fingerprint reader, a centre computer — has its own secret key. When it records something, it signs it with that key. Anyone can check the signature later and know which device said it. Nobody can forge a record for a device they do not have.

Every record is chained to the one before it

Each record carries a fingerprint (a "hash") of the record before it. Change any old record and every record after it stops matching. It is like a numbered register where each page includes a summary of the previous page — tearing one page out is obvious immediately.

Nothing is ever deleted

Records are only ever added. The database itself does not give our software permission to update or delete them. That is not a promise we made in code; it is a permission that does not exist.

Why this matters more than it sounds

In a normal system, if someone deletes an inconvenient step, what is left looks perfectly normal. In ours, deleting a step leaves a hole — the chain stops adding up. A missing record is itself evidence.

Designed but not built

We say this openly rather than letting someone find it. A room monitor (door sensor, presence sensor, people counter) is designed and its firmware is written, but we did not have those sensors. 4How the fingerprint part works

This is the part people ask about most, and the answer is better than they expect.

We never store a fingerprint

The reader keeps the fingerprint inside its own memory. When someone presses a finger, the reader answers with just two things: a slot number and a match score.

So our record says: slot 3 matched, score 187. That is all. No image, no fingerprint data, ever leaves the reader.

If our whole database were stolen tomorrow, no fingerprint could leak from it — because there is no fingerprint in it.

Who slot 3 is — that is kept in a separate list, like a normal staff register. It can be corrected if someone writes it down wrong. The signed records cannot be corrected, and that is the point of keeping the two apart.

5The unlock ceremony

This is the moment that matters: the sealed packet being opened. Here is what happens, in order.

  1. The superintendent presses their finger on the reader.
  2. The reader matches it and reports the slot number and score.
  3. The ESP32 signs that as a record and sends it.
  4. The laptop camera takes one photo — only now, never before.
  5. The observer presses a different finger, within 2 minutes.
  6. A second photo is taken and recorded the same way.
  7. The system checks that two different fingers were used.
  8. All 21 checks run. If every one passes, access is granted.
  9. The paper is decrypted, printed, and the key is destroyed.

The photo is handled carefully

The photo itself never goes into the record. Only its fingerprint — its hash — is stored. Later, anyone can hash the photo and compare it to what was recorded at the time. If they match, that is the photo taken at that moment. If someone swaps the photo, the numbers stop matching.

This keeps the record small, and it means we are not building a photo archive of officials.

The camera only fires on a fingerprint match

Never on a timer. Never on movement. Never because an operator asked. That is a handful of photos per exam of two officials doing one job — not hours of footage of a school corridor. Under India's data protection law, that difference is everything.

6The 21 checks

Two fingerprints are not permission. They are 3 of 21 checks. The others ask:

The system asksExample of a refusal
Was a valid key presented?The key expired six hours ago
Is this device registered to this centre?The device belongs to a different centre
Is this person on the staff list?This finger is not registered to anyone
Is the device at the centre?The phone is 3 km away
Is this the right time to open it?The exam is 18 hours away
Does the seal number match?A different seal is on the packet
Was a photo taken?The camera did not work
Two rules we are proud of

Every check runs, every time. The system does not stop at the first failure. An attempt that fails four checks is a very different event from one that fails on a clock being slightly wrong, and an investigator needs to see all of it.

The attempt is recorded before the answer is given. Even a refusal is written into the chain first. There is no way to try and fail without leaving a trace.

7The key is split four ways

The paper is encrypted. But we do not give the key to one person, because then that person is the weak point.

The key is mathematically split into four pieces. Any three of them rebuild it. Fewer than three are useless — not "harder to use", genuinely useless.

PieceWho holds it
1The exam authority
2A public time-lock service — releases itself at the exam time and not before
3The superintendent, released by their fingerprint
4The observer, released by theirs

So no single person can open a packet early, and no single person can stop an exam by refusing. The time-lock piece is the clever part: before the exam time, that piece does not exist yet. It cannot be leaked, stolen or forced out of anyone, because there is nothing to take.

8What to say in the demo

Three sentences that cover it.

What it adds The record proves what was written down. The hardware proves a specific human body was physically present at the moment the sealed packet was opened — and puts a photograph of that moment into the same unchangeable record.
Why it is limited on purpose The camera fires only during the unlock ceremony, never continuously. We photograph two officials doing one job, not a school corridor. And the fingerprint never leaves the reader — we store a match result, not a biometric.
What it does not do Every one of these sensors can be fooled on its own, and we say so in our own documentation. Their value is that several independent witnesses would all have to be corrupted in the same way, at the same moment — and the one thing that cannot be quietly rewritten is the record of what each of them said at the time.

9What we do not claim

Say these before anyone asks. Someone who finds a weakness you did not mention will assume there are others you are hiding.

Fingerprint readers can be fooled

A cheap optical reader can be fooled by a lifted print. Our records prove a body was present — not that it was the right body. A better reader in real deployment, and the photo is the cross-check.

The device's key can be extracted

With the device in your hands and enough patience, its secret key can be read out of the chip. These devices are tamper-evident, not tamper-proof. Their value is that going silent is itself an alarm.

Two fingers is not proof of two people

The system checks that two different fingerprints were used. If one person registered two of their own fingers under two names, that check passes. That is a failure of the registration process, not of the system — and the two photos would show the same face to anyone who looked.

And the one that matters most

At Hazaribagh, the principal was allowed to be in that room. Fingerprints and sensors catch people who should not be there. They are close to useless against someone who is supposed to be there and betrays that trust.

Which is why the hardware is not the point. The record is the point. It cannot stop that person — but it makes it impossible for them to pretend afterwards that they were never there.

The mistake that costs the most time

Serial lines must cross. The module's transmit pin goes to the pin the ESP32 receives on. Wire transmit to transmit and the module powers up, lights its ring, and never answers a single command — which looks exactly like a dead module.

ESP32 pins are not 5 V tolerant. If the reader runs at 5 V, its transmit line needs a divider before it reaches the board.

Three firmware rules

  • A heartbeat every 30 seconds. The absence of one is itself recorded as an event. Unplugging the device is not a way to go dark — it is a way to raise an alarm.
  • Write the record down before transmitting it. Cutting the power or jamming the Wi-Fi delays a record; it does not erase one.
  • A counter that never repeats. Each record carries a number that only goes up, kept in the chip's own storage. If the ledger holds 41 and then 44, two records existed and did not arrive — which is a different fact from two records never having been made.
Where our build falls short, and says so

No SD card is fitted, so records buffer in memory and do not survive a power cut. No camera is on the board, so the photo comes from the laptop instead. Both conditions are announced into the ledger on every boot rather than quietly absorbed — a device running in a degraded state must be distinguishable in the record from one running properly.

How a record is signed

Two hashes are used, and they answer different questions.

ValueWhat it isWhat it proves
bodyHashHash of the record's own contentsWhich device wrote this, and that the contents have not changed
chainHashHash of (previous chainHash + bodyHash)That nothing has been reordered, inserted or removed since

The device signs only the first. It cannot sign the second, because it does not know what came before — the chain is shared, and the device may have been offline for hours. The ledger fixes the record into its position when it accepts it.

One detail that took real care

Two computers must agree byte-for-byte on what was signed, or every signature fails. Ordinary JSON does not guarantee that — the same data written twice can come out with its fields in a different order, and the signature then no longer matches.

So we use a standard called RFC 8785, which fixes the field order and the number formatting. The ESP32 has no library for it, so the firmware builds its records already in that exact form and refuses to send one that came out wrong. Getting this wrong would produce a device that looks perfectly healthy and whose every record is rejected.

Two rules built into the event types

Some events only a server may write. A device cannot record that its own access was granted, and it cannot record its own silence — a device that could declare itself silent could also decline to.

Some events need two signatures. A handover needs a second signature from a different registered device. Two signatures from one device is one person pressing twice.

The database enforces the important part

The application's database user has no permission to update or delete rows in the event table. The service refuses to start if it finds a connection that does. The guarantee is a database permission, not a promise made in our code — because code can be changed by whoever deploys it.

12Running it

PostgreSQL is the only thing that must be installed. No Docker, no cloud account, nothing paid.

Run thisWhat it starts

tools\monitor-watchdog\run.cmdThe watchdog that notices silence

Three things that catch people out

  • Open the site on localhost. Browsers only allow camera access on localhost or over HTTPS.
  • A phone hotspot changes its network numbering every restart. The address the ESP32 was told is then wrong. It can be corrected from the website without reprogramming the device.
  • Restart the ledger after changing the event definitions. It checks records against the version it started with.
Why the sample data always refuses

The pre-loaded example packages cannot be granted, and that is deliberate, not broken. Their handling windows have closed, and two of the sample centres exist specifically to demonstrate a refusal. A system that granted them would be a system that had stopped checking.
